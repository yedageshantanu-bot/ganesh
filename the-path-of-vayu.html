The fire did not arrive to comfort me. It arrived to burn away the life I thought I was meant to live

— Ganesh Tajane

At first, the transition arrived quietly—not as a sudden vision, a dramatic revelation, or a moment that announced itself as important, but as a subtle, persistent restlessness. On the surface, my life continued exactly as it always had. I taught chess, pursued my personal goals, spent time with family and friends, and tried to move forward in the way the world expects. Yet beneath these ordinary daily routines, a profound question had taken root inside my consciousness: What am I really here for? This question refused to leave, growing stronger with each passing day. Every attempt to distract myself or return to normalcy only seemed to feed the flame, as if an invisible force was slowly, inevitably pulling me toward an unseen destination I could not yet comprehend.

Then, the dreams began. Initially scattered and fleeting, they gradually became more frequent, vivid, and intense until they felt far more real than waking reality itself. I would wake up in the dead of night with my heart racing, carrying deep emotions and energetic currents that lingered for hours. Some nights, I found myself dancing—not as Ganesh, but as something ancient and elemental. I would experience myself moving with an overwhelming, inexplicable force, immersed in a fierce and sacred dance that felt like Tandava itself. Other nights, I would encounter silent yogis and ancient beings whose faces I could not fully recall after waking, yet whose presence felt deeply familiar. Without a word being spoken, I knew that the mysteries they were showing me were of vital importance; I was not merely watching these dreams, I was living them.

As the months passed, the boundary between my inner world and outer reality grew thin. My thoughts carried unusual weight, and my awareness became continuously drawn inward, dominated by the constant rhythm of my breath. Whether during the busyness of the day, the silence of the night, or in moments of deep contemplation, my breath seemed tethered to a deeper intelligence. The closer I moved toward this invisible turning point, the more the inner pressure and the feeling of being called intensified. There were times when I wondered if I was losing control, not out of fear, but because the scale of these experiences exceeded anything I had ever known. My life was no longer moving according to my own plans; a powerful, patient, and ancient force had entered the story, determined to guide me onward.

I did not know where this path was leading, nor what awaited me in the shadows. I only knew that a sacred fire had been lit within my soul—and once lit, it would not let me sleep.

Some dreams visit the mind. Others arrive to change the course of a life

— Ganesh Tajane

Certain moments divide a life into two distinct parts: the before and the after. For me, that definitive boundary line was crossed on an ordinary night, initiated by a dream unlike anything I had ever experienced. At the time, I was living a relatively standard life, where chess occupied most of my waking thoughts and my focus was set on competition, self-improvement, and building a secure future. While spirituality existed quietly in the background, it was far from the center of my universe. Yet, when I opened my eyes the morning after that dream, the vision did not fade. Days turned into weeks, and instead of dissolving like normal dreams, its presence grew stronger and more vivid. It occupied my mind during the hours of daylight and hovered over me as I fell asleep, leaving me with a profound feeling that I could not dismiss.

Compelled to understand the dream's meaning, I began to notice a strong, magnetic inner pull. Glimpses of intuition and subtle synchronicities emerged in my daily life, pointing me toward a specific path. Though I did not fully understand what was happening, I surrendered to this guidance and allowed myself to follow it. Within just a few days, this search led me to a discovery I had never anticipated—something that would quietly but permanently alter the trajectory of my existence. At that early stage, I could not have grasped the true magnitude of what I had found. I only knew that the questions had begun, and they refused to be ignored.

I wondered why this had occurred, why it had happened at this specific moment, and how an unseen hand had so gently yet firmly redirected my path. The answers did not arrive immediately; in truth, they would take years of reflection to fully reveal themselves. Looking back, I can see that the dream was never the destination itself, but rather the first knock on a door that had been waiting for me all along. The full story of that dream, the search that followed, and the spiritual awakening it set into motion is told in detail within the pages of Before I Became Fire.

Some journeys begin with a destination. Others begin with a remembrance

— Ganesh Tajane

Finding the physical Shivling did not bring an end to the questions; if anything, it deepened them. The dream that had consumed my days and disturbed my nights had guided me exactly where it wanted me to go, leading me to find what I had seen in my mind's eye. Yet, instead of immediate clarity, this discovery opened a new, mysterious chapter. The spiritual experiences only intensified. My meditations grew deeper, and the dreams became increasingly vivid, sometimes feeling far more real than waking life itself. I would awaken in the quiet hours carrying intense emotions, profound insights, and memories that seemed to originate from somewhere beyond ordinary human understanding.

I began to perceive that my search for Shiva had not truly started with that single dream. It felt older—older than my questions, older than my ambitions, and perhaps even older than this life itself. I cannot prove these things, nor do I ask anyone to believe them; I can only describe the reality of what I experienced. There was an unmistakable feeling that this journey was not starting, but continuing. Something ancient was unfolding through me, revealing itself layer by layer. In the midst of this inner transformation, another name began to emerge within my consciousness: Vayu. I did not choose it, nor did I invent it. It arrived silently and persisted with a quiet, undeniable certainty.

At first, I did not understand the meaning of the name. But the more I sat with it in silence, the more it felt less like a name and more like a remembrance. It carried a strange, comforting familiarity, as though it belonged to a sacred story that began long before my birth. I cannot fully explain why the name appeared or why it stayed, but over time, I stopped resisting it. The name Vayu became the marker of an inner rebirth—a reminder that something deeper than my personality, ambitions, or social identity was awakening. I did not become someone new; I simply became aware of a presence that had always been there, continuing the journey toward a deeper recognition of the Divine.

A name is not what you are called. A name is what begins to call you.

— Ganesh Tajane

After discovering where the name Vayu had come from, I expected answers. Instead, I was given responsibility. The outward search that had consumed me for months had reached its physical conclusion—the dreams had led me to the Shivling and the signs had revealed the name. Yet, these experiences did not mark the end of the road; they were the beginning of a far deeper commitment. A quiet devotion took shape within me, emerging naturally from the depths of everything I had lived through. The more I reflected on the path behind me, the more I realized that none of it was accidental. The dreams, the visions, the synchronicities, and the people who appeared at the exact moments they were needed were all threads of a single tapestry, guiding me toward a purpose I could not yet fully see.

Gradually, my outer life began to shift. The ambitions that once felt so critical began to fade into the background as my attention turned permanently inward. I found myself drawn to silence, contemplation, and a deeper understanding of my experiences. The mystical occurrences did not cease; if anything, they grew more profound. Along the way, I crossed paths with seekers, saints, yogis, and ordinary people carrying extraordinary wisdom. Some stayed in my life, while others vanished as quickly as they had arrived, but every single encounter deepened my conviction that an unseen intelligence was shaping my path.

Through all of this, a single purpose began to manifest itself repeatedly. What began as a faint feeling evolved into a possibility, and eventually became a calling impossible to ignore: the vision of a temple had entered my life. I did not know how it would happen, nor when, but the idea carried a strange, absolute certainty, as though it had already been decided in some higher realm. The path ahead remained hidden in mist, but for the first time, I knew its direction. And so I continued, no longer searching for answers, but learning to trust the road itself—taking one step, one lesson, and one act of surrender at a time.
