The fire did not arrive to comfort me. It arrived to burn away the life I thought I was meant to live

— Ganesh Tajane

At first, it arrived quietly.

Not as a vision. Not as a revelation. Not as a moment that announced itself as important.

It came as a restlessness.

On the surface, life continued as it always had. I taught chess, pursued my goals, spent time with family and friends, and tried to move forward like everyone else. But beneath the routines of daily life, something had begun to change.

A question had taken root inside me.

What am I really here for?

The question refused to leave. Every day it grew stronger. Every attempt to distract myself only seemed to feed it. It was as if an invisible force was slowly pulling me toward something I could not see and could not understand.

Then the dreams began.

At first they were scattered and difficult to remember. But as the months passed, they became more frequent, more vivid, and more intense.

These were not ordinary dreams.

They felt more real than reality itself.

I would wake up with my heart racing, carrying emotions that stayed with me long after the dream had ended. Sometimes it felt as though I had not been dreaming at all, but had stepped into another world and returned before dawn.

Some nights I found myself dancing.

Not as Ganesh.

Something else.

Something ancient.

I would experience myself moving with a force I could not explain, immersed in a fierce and overwhelming dance that felt like Tandava itself. The intensity of those moments was difficult to put into words. Even after waking, the energy remained in my body for hours.

Other nights, I would encounter figures I had never seen before.

Yogis.

Silent beings.

Men whose faces I could not fully remember after waking, yet whose presence felt deeply familiar.

Sometimes they spoke.

Sometimes they simply looked at me.

And somehow, without fully understanding why, I knew that what they were showing me was important.

The strange part was not the dreams themselves.

The strange part was how real they felt.

I was not watching them.

I was living them.

As time passed, the experiences became stronger.

The boundary between my inner world and outer world seemed to grow thinner. Thoughts that once came and went now carried unusual weight. My awareness became increasingly drawn inward.

And then there was the breath.

The breath began to dominate everything.

I noticed it constantly.

During the day.

Late at night.

In moments of silence.

In moments of activity. It was as though my attention was being pulled toward it against my will. The rhythm of my breathing seemed connected to something deeper that I could not yet understand.

The closer I moved toward a moment I could not see, the stronger everything became.

The dreams intensified.

The inner pressure intensified.

The feeling of being called intensified.

There were times when I wondered if I was losing control. Not because I was afraid, but because the experiences felt larger than anything I had encountered before. Life was no longer moving according to my plans. Something else had entered the story.

Something powerful.

Something patient.

Something that seemed determined to guide me whether I was ready or not.

I did not know where it was leading.

I did not know what awaited me.

I only knew one thing.

The fire had been lit.

And once it was lit, it would not let me sleep.

I thought I was
searching for answers.
Only later did I
realize that something
far greater was
searching for me.

Some dreams visit the mind. Others arrive to change the course of a life

— Ganesh Tajane

Some moments divide a life into two parts: before and after.

For me, that moment arrived quietly.

At the time, I was living what appeared to be an ordinary life. Chess occupied most of my thoughts. I was focused on improvement, competition, and building a future. Spirituality was present in the background, but it was not yet the center of my world.

Then came a dream.

It was unlike anything I had experienced before.

When I opened my eyes the next morning, the dream remained with me. Days passed, yet it refused to leave. Most dreams fade with time; this one only grew stronger. I found myself thinking about it constantly. It occupied my mind during the day and followed me into the night.

There was something about it that I could not dismiss.

I felt compelled to understand it.

In the days that followed, I began experiencing an unusual inner pull—subtle impressions, glimpses, and intuitions that seemed to point me in a particular direction. I did not fully understand what was happening, but I felt drawn to follow them.

Within a few days, that search led me to something I had never expected to find.

Something that would quietly alter the course of my life.

At the time, I could not have imagined the significance of that discovery. I only knew that the questions had begun, and they were not going away.

Why had this happened?

Why now?

And why did it feel as though an unseen hand had gently redirected my path?

The answers did not arrive immediately. In fact, they would take years to unfold.

Looking back, I can see that the dream was not the destination.

It was the first knock on a door that had been waiting for me all along.

The full story of that dream, the search that followed, and everything it set into motion is told in Before I Became Fire.

I thought I had
awakened from the dream.
Years later, I realized
the dream had
awakened me.

Some journeys begin with a destination. Others begin with a remembrance

— Ganesh Tajane

Finding the Shivling did not bring an end to the questions.

If anything, it deepened them.

The dream that had consumed my days and disturbed my nights had led me exactly where it wanted me to go. Within days, I found what I had seen. Yet instead of clarity, a new chapter began to unfold.

The experiences continued.

Meditations became deeper. Dreams became more vivid. At times they felt more real than waking life itself. There were nights when I would awaken carrying emotions, insights, and memories that seemed to come from somewhere beyond ordinary understanding.

Something was changing within me.

I began to feel that my search for Shiva had not truly begun with that dream. It felt older than that. Older than my questions, older than my ambitions, perhaps even older than this life itself.

I cannot prove such things, nor do I ask anyone to believe them.

I can only describe what I experienced.

There was an unmistakable feeling that this journey was not starting—it was continuing. Something ancient seemed to be unfolding through me, revealing itself layer by layer. The more I surrendered to it, the more I realized that I was not creating the path. I was simply walking a road that had been waiting for me all along.

During this period, another name began to emerge within my consciousness.

Vayu.

I did not choose it. I did not invent it. It arrived quietly and persisted with a certainty I could not ignore.

At first, I did not understand its meaning.

But the more I sat with it, the more it felt less like a name and more like a remembrance. It carried a strange familiarity, as though it belonged to a story that had begun long before I became aware of it.

I cannot fully explain why that name appeared, nor can I explain why it stayed.

I only know that over time, I stopped resisting it.

The name became a marker of an inner transformation—a reminder that something deeper than personality, ambition, or identity was awakening within me.

I did not become someone new.

I became more aware of something that had always been there.

And so the journey continued—not toward a destination, but toward a deeper recognition of what had been calling me all along.

I was not becoming
someone else.
I was remembering
who I had
always been.

A name is not what you are called. A name is what begins to call you.

— Ganesh Tajane

After understanding where the name Vayu had come from, I expected answers.

Instead, I was given responsibility.

The search that had consumed me for months was over. The dreams had led me to the Shivling. The signs had revealed the name. Yet rather than bringing an end to the journey, these experiences marked the beginning of something much deeper.

A quiet commitment began to take shape within me.

Not a commitment forced upon me, but one that emerged naturally from everything I had experienced.

The more I reflected on the path behind me, the more I felt that none of it had been accidental. The dreams, the visions, the synchronicities, the people who appeared at unexpected moments—each seemed to be guiding me toward something I could not yet fully see.

Life gradually began to change.

The ambitions that once felt important started fading into the background. My attention turned inward. I found myself drawn toward silence, contemplation, and a deeper understanding of why these experiences had entered my life in the first place.

The mystical experiences did not stop.

If anything, they became more profound.

Along the way, I met seekers, saints, yogis, and ordinary people carrying extraordinary wisdom. Some remained in my life. Others crossed my path briefly before disappearing. Yet every encounter deepened the feeling that an unseen intelligence was shaping the journey.

And through it all, a single purpose began appearing again and again.

At first it was only a feeling.

Then it became a possibility.

Then it became something impossible to ignore.

The vision of a temple had entered my life.

I did not know how it would happen.

I did not know when.

I only knew that the idea carried a strange certainty, as though it had already been decided somewhere beyond my understanding.

The path ahead remained hidden.

But for the first time, I could sense its direction.

And so I continued—not searching for answers anymore, but learning to trust the road itself.

One step.

One lesson.

One act of surrender at a time.

Sometimes the next chapter
of your life begins
the moment you stop
asking where the
path leads and
simply choose to walk it.
