The purpose of a temple is not to bring God closer to man, but to bring man closer to himself

— Ganesh Tajane

Why do we build temples? If the Divine is everywhere, why create a sacred place? Why spend centuries carving mountains into shrines, raising towering temples, and preserving them generation after generation? Surely, it is not because God needs a home. Temples were never built for stone; they were built for the human beings.

Life constantly pulls us outward. Every day, we chase success, responsibilities, relationships, ambitions, and countless distractions. In that endless movement, it is easy to lose the one thing that matters most—ourselves. A temple exists to reverse that journey. It invites us to turn inward before stepping back into the world.

A temple is not merely a place to pray. It is a carefully created space where the mind can become quiet, where the heart can become receptive, and where one can behold the form of the Divine with complete attention. The purpose is not simply to see the deity; the purpose is to allow the deity to become alive within you.

When you leave the temple, you are meant to carry that presence into your thoughts, your words, and your actions. The silence you experienced inside should walk with you outside. The grace you felt before the deity should become the way you treat another human being. The clarity you discovered within should guide every decision you make. Then every action becomes meaningful, every word carries awareness, and every step becomes an offering towards the well-being of others.

That is why a temple matters. Not because the Divine is confined within its walls, but because the human mind often needs a sacred space to remember what it truly is. A temple is not an escape from life; it is where we prepare ourselves to live life consciously. Perhaps that is why our ancestors built temples with such devotion. They were not only preserving faith; they were preserving a way of transforming human consciousness.

The more I reflected on this, the more I realized that my journey was never about searching for a temple. It was about discovering why one must first be built within.
